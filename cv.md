**Colin Ophus**  
Associate Professor, Materials Science and Engineering, Stanford University  
[cophus@stanford.edu](mailto:cophus@stanford.edu) · [Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.com/citations?hl=en&user=h5MPYJAAAAAJ&view_op=list_works&sortby=pubdate) · [ORCID](https://orcid.org/0000-0003-2348-8558) · [GitHub](https://github.com/cophus) · [Group website](https://colab.stanford.edu/)

## Research Interests

TEM/STEM; 4D-STEM; electron ptychography; atomic electron tomography (AET); strain, orientation, and phase mapping; computational imaging and inverse problems with physics-based and generative ML priors; in situ and time-resolved methods; instrumentation and acquisition workflows; open-source simulation and analysis software.

## Professional Experience

- **2024–present** · **Associate Professor**, [Materials Science and Engineering](https://mse.stanford.edu/), Stanford University.  
  **L&S Family Center Fellow in Energy and Sustainability**, [Precourt Institute for Energy](https://energy.stanford.edu/), Stanford University.
  - Research & teaching in materials science, data science, & electron microscopy.
- **2016–2024** · **Staff Scientist**, [Molecular Foundry](http://foundry.lbl.gov/), [Berkeley National Lab](http://www.lbl.gov/), Berkeley, CA.
  - Led an independent research program in materials science and microscopy, including writing grant applications both individually and in collaborations.
  - Ran a user program focused on computational imaging and electron microscopy. Taught microscopy, data analysis, and computational analysis.
- **2012–2016** · **Project Scientist**, [NCEM](http://foundry.lbl.gov/), [Berkeley National Lab](http://www.lbl.gov/), Berkeley, CA, USA.
  - Similar duties to Staff Scientist position.
- **2010–2012** · **Materials Postdoctoral Fellow**, [National Center for Electron Microscopy](http://foundry.lbl.gov/facilities/ncem/), [Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory](http://www.lbl.gov/), Berkeley, CA, USA.
  - Materials science including electron microscopy and alloy design.
  - First principles simulation and computational thermodynamics.
